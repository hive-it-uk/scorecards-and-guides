Analyse a stack

Add technologies from any category to see a combined picture. For self-hosted open-source tools, the environmental criteria are inherited from whichever host you pick — so add a host to complete the picture.

How to analyse a stack
  1. Start with a preset, or build your own. Pick a preset stack below, or choose technologies one at a time from the "Add a technology" list.
  2. Add your hosting provider. Self-hosted tools (frameworks, databases, CMSs) take their environmental scores from whichever host runs them — without a host, those criteria are left out rather than guessed.
  3. Read the results in order. Red flags first, then the three pillar scores with their evidence coverage, then the overall score. A weak-pillar warning means one pillar is dragging the result down — look at that pillar before anything else.
  4. Adjust to match your priorities. The scoring controls above let you weight the pillars differently and set where you deploy (UK, EU or US); some scores change with location.
  5. Estimate the environmental impact. Enter your monthly page impressions and page weight in the calculator further down to see carbon and water figures for the whole stack.

      About the method

      How we score

      The framework: 13 criteria

      Built by Hive IT as applied research. Scores use a 0–4 scale across thirteen criteria in three pillars, then normalised to 0–100. Red flags are reported separately and never blended into scores. “Unknown” means we found no information and is scored zero by default — a deliberately cautious choice. Many entries are open-source projects with no corporate environmental disclosure, which is itself a finding, not a gap in this scorecard.

      Accessibility: built to WCAG 2.2 AA. British English and plain language throughout.
